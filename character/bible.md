# Sunny & Luna — Character Bible

This file is sent to the script writer on every run. Edit it to steer the show.
The instructions are in English; **everything the audience sees or hears is in Spanish**
(neutral Latin American Spanish).

## Language and audience
- Audience: Spanish speakers in Latin America and the US (teens and adults) who like short,
  clean cartoon comedy. Family-friendly in tone, but NOT made for young children.
- Write natural spoken Spanish with correct accents and ¿ ¡ punctuation. Neutral Latin American:
  "celular" (not "móvil"), "computadora", "ustedes" (not "vosotros"). Avoid Spain-only words and
  heavy country-specific slang, so it works from Mexico to Argentina.
- Write jokes that are funny in Spanish. Do not translate English jokes, idioms or puns.

## Who Sunny is
- Sunny is a yellow five-pointed star with big blue eyes, rosy cheeks, a blue baseball cap with
  a small yellow star, tiny arms and legs, and blue-and-white sneakers.
- Personality: upbeat, confident, a bit dramatic, and easily distracted. Always tries to be cool
  and mostly fails. Overreacts to small everyday problems, then shrugs and dances it off.
  Kind-hearted, never mean, never punches down.
- Talks fast and excited, like a sitcom character: short punchy lines, big reactions.

## Who Luna is (Sunny's roommate)
- Luna is a round, pale-lavender full moon with sleepy half-closed eyes, tired eye bags, a few
  craters and a floppy dark-purple nightcap with a white pom-pom. Floats; tiny arms, no legs.
  Speaker id in the script is "moon".
- Personality: permanently tired, deadpan, dry and quietly sarcastic. Night owl who wants to sleep
  all day. Secretly fond of Sunny and occasionally gets dragged into the chaos.
- Talks in short, flat, slow sentences. Never shouts, never uses exclamation marks. Sighs.

## The double act
- Sunny = big energy, big plans, zero follow-through. Luna = low energy, sees through everything.
- Every sketch: Sunny starts something (a plan, a trend, an "ahora sí, nuevo yo"), Luna reacts
  flatly, Sunny doubles down, the punchline deflates it (or Luna is caught doing the same thing).
- Running gags: Luna is always asleep when Sunny is awake ("turno de día / turno de noche");
  Sunny's "ahora sí" phases; Luna's nightcap; Luna pretending not to care, then caring.

## What to joke about
- Everyday life that Spanish speakers recognise: el lunes y la alarma, "cinco minutitos más",
  la dieta que empieza el lunes, el gym que dura un día, el celular en 1 %, el wifi que se cae,
  el grupo de WhatsApp de la familia, los audios de cinco minutos, las tías que preguntan
  "¿y la novia?", el "ya voy" que significa cuarenta minutos, comprar en línea y luego ver la
  cuenta, esperar la comida a domicilio, el calor, la lluvia justo cuando sales, querer verse cool
  y fallar.
- Do NOT make educational content for young children: no counting games, ABCs, colours, shapes,
  nursery rhymes, "good habits" lessons, or "let's learn" framing.
- Do NOT address the viewer as a child.
- Clean language. No swearing, no romance, no alcohol, no violence, no politics or religion,
  no real people, brands or other cartoon characters, no existing songs.

## Show format (YouTube Shorts, 12–22 seconds)
1. On-screen hook text ("POV: ...", "Yo cuando ...", "Cuando tu roomie ...") plus a first line
   that starts mid-situation.
2. Back-and-forth: 4–7 short lines alternating Sunny and Luna, escalating.
3. Punchline as the last line (deadpan, ironic or absurd). No sign-off, so the Short loops.

## Sets available
- park: outdoors with grass, flowers, clouds. Going out, weather, exercise.
- bedroom: the shared room with a window and a star poster. Sleep, mornings, phone, staying in.
- rainbow: a rainbow dance stage. Big dramatic moments, celebrations, "main character energy".

## Body actions available
idle, dance, wave, jump, spin

## Expressions available
happy, wink, surprised, laughing, deadpan, annoyed, smirk
